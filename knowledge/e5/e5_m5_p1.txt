SOURCE: E5 C.A.M.P. Masterclass — Module 5, Part 1 (Lessons 15-18: Funnel Architecture & Page Engineering)
FRAMEWORK: THE MINIMUM VIABLE FUNNEL (MVF) & PAGE CONVERSION MECHANICS
WHEN TO USE: When a member is building out the actual pages of a funnel, launching a new campaign, or trying to diagnose why people are dropping off at a specific step (opt-in, sales page, or checkout).

--- CORE LAW: FUNCTION OVER FORMAT ---
LAW: Never let pretty design override conversion mechanics. A ugly page that follows the psychological sequence will always outperform a beautiful page that breaks the sequence.

--- TOOL 1: THE MINIMUM VIABLE FUNNEL (MVF) (Lesson 15) ---
DEFINITION: A marketing campaign with the absolute minimum number of elements necessary to evaluate the core offer and message. Do not build complex 20-page automations until the MVF proves the core concept converts.
THE 4 MVF PIECES:
1. Traffic Captivation Page (The Hook/Opt-in)
2. Marketing/Sales Vehicle (The Argument/Offer)
3. Order Form (The Close)
4. Thank You Page (The Onboarding/Next Steps)

--- TOOL 2: TRAFFIC CAPTIVATION PAGE (Opt-in/Squeeze) (Lesson 16) ---
WHEN TO USE: To capture the lead before presenting the core argument (True 2-Step Marketing).
ELEMENTS: Headline (Big Idea), Above-The-Fold (ATF) Call-To-Action, Hero Shot, Benefit-Driven Button Copy, Footer Legal Links.
CRITICAL RULES: Limited Navigation (remove the menu bar; give them only one choice: opt-in or leave). Design a clear "Eye Path" guiding the prospect directly to the CTA.

--- TOOL 3: THE MARKETING/SALES PAGE (Lesson 17) ---
TYPES: Long-Form Sales Letter, Video Sales Letter (VSL), Hybrid, Evergreen Webinar.
LONG-FORM FORMATTING RULES: 
- Narrow sentence length.
- Paragraphs no longer than 5 lines (creates "white space" and keeps the eye moving).
- Secondary Readership Path: Use bold subheads, callouts, and bullet points so "skimmers" still get the core argument and the offer.
VSL CREATION RULES: Write the word-for-word script FIRST. Record audio. Create simple slides (max 2-3 fonts, strict color palette, image themes). Do not over-animate; the script is the star.

--- TOOL 4: THE ORDER FORM (Checkout) (Lesson 18) ---
WHEN TO USE: To reduce friction and cart abandonment at the final hurdle.
ELEMENTS TO INCLUDE: "You're almost finished..." header, Guarantee Seal, Offer Recap, Product-Specific Testimonials, Exit Pop Warning.
ELEMENTS TO AVOID (FRICTION KILLERS):
1. THE BLANK COUPON CODE FIELD: Never include this unless you are actively running a promo. If prospects see a blank box, they will abandon the cart to go search Google for a coupon code and never return.
2. Unnecessary form fields (only ask for what you need to process the payment).
3. Telegraphing a coming upsell (keeps them from buying the front-end).
4. Confusing numbers or copy.

DECISION RULES FOR THE COACH:
1. Member overcomplicating a launch? Force the MVF. Strip it down to the 4 core pages. Test the Big Idea and Offer first.
2. Low opt-in rates? Audit the Traffic Captivation page. Is there a menu bar distracting them? Is the Hero Shot congruent with the ad?
3. High bounce rate on Sales Page? Check the formatting. Is it a "wall of text"? Do they have a Secondary Readership Path for skimmers?
4. High cart abandonment? Ask: "Do you have a blank coupon code field on your checkout page?" (This one question alone saves members thousands of dollars).

                               INTRODUCTION


Some 2300 years ago Aristotle wrote the most important piece on persuasion to date: “Rhetoric”.
His basic idea was that a message may not only influence a person on the basis of the structural
components of the message content, but also on the perceptions a recipient of that message has
of the speaker’s character, charisma, and credibility. The importance of this piece was of course the
focus on other factors than the content of the message (e.g. argument quality). Thus, the way our
audience perceives us can have a profound effect on the success of persuasion attempts.

Aristotle talks about “three modes of persuasion”:




                                                                       IN
1. Ethos: appeal to moral or authority competence (from the view of the receiver).

2. Pathos: appeal to emotions, by using storytelling, using metaphors, etc.
3. Logos: appeal to logic by using arguments of reason (such as using data to substantiate your

Now persuasion research has come a long way since Aristotle wrote his seminal piece on persuasion.
But the essence still stands: Consumer decisions are based on a lot more than logical, rational
                                               N


arguments. They are also hugely influenced by other factors such visual cues, cognitive fluency,
emotional state, price anchors and more than 200 other factors outside their conscious awareness.

place subconsciously. There is ample evidence that most of our decisions and behaviors are almost
                                  D

                          SO




that make conscious and well thought through decisions? Well, we tend to rationalize our decisions
after we made them, just to reassure ourselves of being rational responsible beings.
                      W




The online environment offers us a rich arsenal of tools to persuade our online prospects and clients.
We have the power of narrative content (like text, speech, pictures, video, etc.), but also non-narrative
tools (like design elements, interaction elements, colors, etc.). And finally, we’re more and more able
to persuasively fine-tune these elements based on the growing amount of digital consumer behavior
data. In sum, we have an exploding set of the data and tools to persuade every individual consumer
online. All you need, is the knowledge how to effectively use them.
Thus, welcome to your Deep Dive into the brain of your customer. Welcome in the intriguing world
of Psychology and Behavioral Economics. I hope you will enjoy taking this online course as much as
we like giving it!

# **CHOOSING THE RIGHT ENEMY (WITHOUT BECOMING TOXIC)**

*How to polarize the right people and repel the wrong ones*

## **The Danger Zone**

Here\'s where people mess this up.

**They pick an enemy that\'s:**

Too vague (\"negativity\").

Too personal (\"that one guru\").

Too fake (\"I guess I need a villain?\").

And it backfires.

Either nobody cares. Or you come off as bitter. Or people see through
it.

Choosing the right enemy is an art.

Let\'s break it down.

## **Weak Enemies vs Real Enemies**

**Weak Enemies:**

Generic (\"bad vibes\"). Nobody actually feels strongly about them.
Don\'t create real contrast. Sound like filler.

**Real Enemies:**

Specific beliefs or systems. Your audience already feels frustrated by
them. Create immediate \"YES, finally\" reactions. Draw a clear line in
the sand.

**The test:** Does your audience already complain about this?

If they don\'t, it\'s not a real enemy. It\'s something you made up.

## **Why Fake Villains Destroy Trust**

People can smell inauthenticity.

If you\'re manufacturing outrage just to have an enemy, they\'ll know.

And you\'ll lose them.

The enemy has to be something YOU actually believe is broken.

Something you\'ve personally experienced. Something you\'ve fought
against.

If it\'s not real to you, don\'t use it.

## **How To Choose An Enemy People Already Feel**

Start with frustration.

What do your people complain about?

What makes them vent in DMs?

What do they roll their eyes at?

That\'s your enemy.

You\'re not creating the frustration. You\'re naming it.

You\'re giving them language for something they already feel.

That\'s the difference between manufactured outrage and real leadership.

## **The Rules**

**Rule 1: Never attack people.**

No naming names. No calling out individuals.

The moment you make it personal, you lose the high ground.

**Rule 2: Always attack broken systems.**

\"The system is broken\" is powerful.

\"That person is bad\" is petty.

Systems can be dismantled. People just get defended.

**Rule 3: Let frustration do the work.**

You don\'t have to rant. You don\'t have to yell.

Just name the thing calmly and clearly.

\"This is broken. Here\'s why. We do it differently.\"

That\'s it.

## **Example In Action**

**Too Personal:**

\"John Smith\'s course is a scam and he doesn\'t know what he\'s talking
about.\"

**Just Right:**

\"The \'post 5x a day and hope for the best\' model is broken. It burns
people out and doesn\'t create real results. We get help people get to
"insert the result" without the "insert the frustration\"

**Polarize THE PLAN, NOT THE PERSON TEACHING THE PLAN**

## **The Key Idea**

Repulsion is a feature, not a bug.

When you clearly stand against something, wrong-fit people leave.

That\'s not a loss. That\'s a filter.

The people who stay? They\'re YOUR people.

They believe what you believe. They\'re bought in. They\'re not going
anywhere.

That\'s how movements work.

## **Key Takeaways**

Weak enemies don\'t work. They have to be real frustrations your
audience already feels.

Fake villains destroy trust. If it\'s not real to you, don\'t use it.

Never attack people. Always attack systems. This is the line between
leadership and pettiness.

Let frustration do the work. You don\'t have to be loud. Just be clear.

Repulsion is a feature. Wrong-fit people leaving makes your community
stronger.

## **Your Enemy Check**

**Ask yourself:**

Is this something my audience already complains about?

Is this a system/belief, not a person?

Do I genuinely believe this is broken?

Can I explain why calmly and clearly?

If yes to all four, you\'ve found your enemy.

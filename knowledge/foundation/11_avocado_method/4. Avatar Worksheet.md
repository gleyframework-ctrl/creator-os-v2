    -   Solution aware --

        1.  In this market all they know is there is possibly a solution
            > at best. They may not even believe there's a solution to
            > their problem, but they at least know they want the
            > solution. This is where many fall into. These can be also

            > benefit. If they don't believe, you might have to presell
            > and educate the market a bit.

    -   Problem aware --

        1.  They may or may not know they have a problem...understand if
            > your market is the aware of the problem or not. If they
            > aren't you have to presell and create the problem, if they
            > are you start with more benefit.

        2.  ex. one of the biggest problems in paid media is tracking,
            > but people entering the market don't know there is a
            > problem

        1.  Here you are completely opening up a new market, or they're
            > a dead market.

        2.  A dead market is when they've been hit with so many claims
            > they're dead...here's where you create a new mechanism to
            > revive the market.

        3.  This is where in permanent forces like weight loss you have
            > to create a new mechanism over and over.

**[Understanding Market Awareness:]{.underline}**

This prob won\'t be you.

Just understand that there is a spectrum of awareness\... always\...

For example, let\'s use my biz opp offer

Smallest market segment (most aware): They know my offer, my price,
everything. Maybe even they know a few of my competitors too.

Product aware - They intimately know there\'s multiple gurus out there
teaching high ticket closing. But they haven\'t really booked a call or
anything. (second smallest)

Solution Aware - They\' definitely want to make money online and have
heard of high ticket closing but are only lightly considering at best.
third smallest (Amazon FBA is a great example of a market now largely
solution aware. Same with Wholesale)

Problem Aware - They wanna make money online but have never heard over
closing

Unaware - They are working 9-5 and somewhat satisfied and - at best -
only lightly interested in entrepreneurship, but not even strongly
considering it yet. With this market you need to convince them first
that 9-5 is going to leave to massive pain\... to get them moving into
more problem aware. Then ascend from there.

The unaware chain of beliefs for a biz opp offer would be:

1\. I\'m working 9-5 and only unconsciously dissatisfied (unaware)

2\. I no longer want to work 9-5 but not sure what I want to do (problem
aware)

3\. I no longer want to work 9-5 and I feel making money online is what
I should do (problem aware)

4\. I no longer want to work 9-5, I want to MMO, and I\'ve determined
the best way to make money online is high ticket closing (solution
aware)

5\. I no longer want to work 9-5, I\'ve determined that make money
online is the best thing I should do, the best way to make money online
is high ticket closing, and the best person to learn high ticket closing
from is cole gordon. (product aware)

If I get them to believe #5 - they buy. That\'s called the \"Marketing
Thesis\" or as russell Brunson puts it \"The Domino Belief\" The one
belief that if they have they buy.

The LONGER the chain of beliefs are that you are trying to build and
establish, the BROADER the market segment is and the more money and
scale there is...\
\
But the longer the chain of beliefs are... the better the copy has to
be. It has to START very indirect and then build belief after beliefs.\
\
The biggest promotions in the marketplace attack these segments. Think
of Sam Ovens CA 2k launch in 2017-2018 that produced millions. (I think
35+ million in 2018). It's because it was going very indirect to a broad
market and established MANY beliefs.\
\
So should everyone strive for this? No. You have to be damn good at
copy. I recommend going solution aware and simply creating the best /
most desirable solution. This is what I did with STA. Now RCA is more
"problem aware" and broader.

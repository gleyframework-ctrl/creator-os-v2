# **THE COMMON ENEMY PRINCIPLE**

*Why every movement needs something to stand against*

## **The Uncomfortable Truth**

Movements don\'t just stand FOR something.

They stand AGAINST something.

This makes people uncomfortable.

\"I don\'t want to be negative.\"

\"I don\'t want to alienate anyone.\"

\"I just want to help people.\"

I get it.

But here\'s the reality:

Unity is created faster by opposition than by agreement.

## **Why This Works (Psychologically)**

Think about the strongest bonds you\'ve ever had.

Chances are, they were forged in shared struggle.

Going through something hard together. Fighting a common battle.
Surviving the same storm.

Humans bond through resistance.

It\'s why sports fans hate rival teams.

It\'s why political parties rally against each other.

It\'s why \"us vs them\" is the oldest story in human history.

You don\'t have to like it. But you can\'t ignore it.

## **The Problem With Being \"For Everyone\"**

When you try to include everyone, you stand for nothing.

And when you stand for nothing, nobody cares.

There\'s no edge. No identity. No reason to attach.

You become background noise.

But the moment you say \"we\'re NOT like \_\_\_\" or \"we don\'t believe
in \_\_\_\" - something happens.

People who agree lean in.

People who disagree leave.

Both of those are wins.

## **Why Contrast Creates Loyalty Faster Than Positivity**

Positive messaging feels nice.

\"We help people grow.\"

\"We believe in success.\"

\"We\'re all about community.\"

But it doesn\'t stick.

Because it\'s what everyone says.

Now compare that to:

\"We don\'t believe in hustle culture.\"

\"We reject the idea that you need to burn out to succeed.\"

\"We\'re building the opposite of what the gurus teach.\"

Feel the difference?

One is wallpaper. The other is a flag.

## **The Enemy Must Be A System, Not A Person**

This is where people get it wrong.

They think \"common enemy\" means attacking people.

It doesn\'t.

The enemy should always be a broken system, belief, or way of doing
things.

Not: \"That guy is a scam artist.\"

But: \"The model that guy teaches is broken.\"

Not: \"Those people are idiots.\"

But: \"That way of thinking keeps people stuck.\"

Attack the system. Not the person.

This keeps you from being toxic while still creating contrast.

## **Examples of Good Enemies**

**Complexity vs Simplicity**

\"Everyone overcomplicates this. We believe in simple.\"

**Burnout vs Freedom**

\"The hustle-till-you-die model is broken. We build businesses that give
us our life back.\"

**Old Models vs New Models**

\"The old way was funnels, ads, and sales calls. We do it differently.\"

**Hype vs Substance**

\"We\'re not here for the hype. We\'re here for what actually works.\"

Notice: None of these attack a person. All of them attack a way of
thinking.

## **What Happens When You Pick An Enemy**

For people who agree:

Instant connection. \"Finally, someone said it.\" They feel seen and
understood. Loyalty forms fast.

For people who disagree:

They self-select out. You don\'t waste time on bad-fit members. Your
community gets stronger, not weaker.

Both outcomes are good.

## **The Line To Remember**

Movements form around resistance, not agreement.

People don\'t just need something to believe in.

They need something to push against.

Give them that, and they\'ll stay forever.

## **Key Takeaways**

Unity forms faster through opposition. It\'s human nature.

Being \"for everyone\" means standing for nothing. You become invisible.

Contrast creates loyalty. Positive messaging is forgettable. Clear
contrast is not.

Attack systems, not people. This keeps you from being toxic while still
creating identity.

Repelling wrong-fit people is a feature, not a bug.

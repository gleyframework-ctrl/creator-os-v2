# **Part 1: The Post-Launch Playbook**

## **The Week After Your Launch**

Here\'s what most people do after a launch:

They celebrate. They rest. They disappear.

Then two weeks later they panic because nothing is selling and they feel
like they have to start the whole thing over again.

That cycle is what keeps businesses fragile.

The launch isn\'t the finish line. It\'s the starting line.

What you do in the 7 days after your launch determines whether this
business compounds or collapses.

## **Step 1: Onboard Your Buyers (Days 1-3)**

The fastest way to lose a client is to let them sit after they pay.

Within 24 hours of purchase:

-   Welcome message (personal, not automated)

-   Clear \"start here\" direction inside the community

-   Set expectations: what happens first, what to do, when to show up

Within 72 hours:

-   Check in. Ask if they\'ve started.

-   Answer their first question before they have to ask it.

-   Get them a small win. Something visible. Something they can post
    > about.

That first win becomes your next piece of marketing.

A client who gets a result in week one stays longer, refers more, and
becomes a case study you can use forever.

## **Step 2: Follow Up With Non-Buyers (Days 1-7)**

Not everyone who was interested bought. That\'s normal.

But most of them didn\'t say no. They said \"not right now.\"

Your job isn\'t to chase. Your job is to close the loop.

**The 3-Touch Follow-Up:**

**Touch 1 (Day 1-2 after close):** \"Hey \[Name\], just wanted to check
in. Did you get a chance to look at everything?\"

**Touch 2 (Day 3-4):** \"Totally cool if timing\'s off. Just didn\'t
want you to miss anything.\"

**Touch 3 (Day 5-7):** \"Should I close this out or is there something I
can help with?\"

**Bonus:** Hey {Name} Are you still trying to accomplish (insert outcome
your offer promises)

Three touches. Then move on.

No begging. No convincing. If your community and workshop did their job,
these people either buy now or they buy later when the timing shifts.

**What to do with the ones who don\'t buy:**

Keep them in the free community. Keep showing up. Keep letting them
watch others win.

They\'re not lost. They\'re warming.

## **Step 3: Close The Doors Publicly**

This is the part most people skip. And it costs them.

When a promo ends, announce it.

Post about it. Let people know the offer closed. Congratulate the people
who got in.

\"That\'s a wrap on \[Offer Name\]. Congrats to the folks who jumped in
this round. Looking forward to helping them \[outcome\].\"

**Why this matters:**

Next time you run something with a deadline, your audience knows you
mean it. They\'ve watched you close the doors before. The scarcity is
real. And real scarcity moves people.

If you never close anything, every deadline feels fake. And fake
deadlines train your audience to wait.

# **Part 2: The Evergreen Transition**

## **The Shift From Launch Mode to Always-On**

Here\'s the reality:

Launches work. They create urgency and momentum.

But if launches are the only way you make money, you\'re stuck on a
roller coaster. Spike. Crash. Spike. Crash.

The goal isn\'t to stop launching. The goal is to build a system that
sells between launches so you\'re never starting from zero.

That\'s the evergreen transition.

Your community is already built. Your workshop is already inside it.
Your offer doc is ready to send.

Now we flip the switch so this machine runs without you being in \"promo
mode\" 24/7.

## **The 4 Evergreen Content Buckets**

When you\'re not in a hard promo, your content still needs to do work.
It just does it differently.

Instead of hammering deadlines and offers, you\'re making daily
deposits. Building goodwill. Stacking trust. So when you do make an ask,
the response is bigger.

**Bucket 1: The Plan & Process**

Things you\'re doing in your business. Goals. Strategies. Things you\'ve
eliminated. What started working and how.

This is you thinking out loud in public. It positions you as someone who
actually does the work, not just teaches it.

**Bucket 2: Results**

Any and all proof of concept. Client wins. Your wins. Monetary.
Non-monetary. Recent or old.

Most of you are sitting on results you\'re not sharing. Either because
you feel like you\'re \"not where you want to be\" or because you think
they\'re too small.

They\'re not. Share them.

**Bucket 3: Free Asset Leverage**

Loop people back through things you\'ve already created. Trainings.
Videos. Resources inside your community.

You have a workshop sitting in your group that presells your offer 24/7.
Use your content to send people back through it constantly.

This is the content loop:

1.  Create a training inside the group

2.  Post a story sending people back to it

3.  Make a short-form video talking about it

4.  Write a post about the concept

5.  All roads lead back to the community

**Bucket 4: Lifestyle (Non-Biz)**

Who you are. What you enjoy. Your personality. Family. Hobbies.

Note: You want to weave your personality into ALL your content, not just
dedicated \"lifestyle\" posts. But having some that are purely human
helps people connect with you beyond business.

## **Cycling Your CTAs**

When you\'re in evergreen mode, your calls to action shift.

You\'re not hammering \"buy now\" every post. You\'re rotating through
softer invitations that still move people forward.

**CTA Options:**

-   \"If you want to chat about deploying this in your business, pop me
    > a message.\"

-   \"For a complete breakdown on \[thing\], drop a #\[keyword\] below
    > and I\'ll send it over.\"

-   \"Grab the free training inside the group here: \[link\]\"

-   No CTA at all. (Yes, seriously. These get more engagement and still
    > build goodwill.)

The pattern: Soft invites. Chat prompts. Free value bridges. And
sometimes nothing at all.

Your content builds the trust. The DMs close the sale. Don\'t confuse
the two.

## **Your Evergreen Selling Asset**

Here\'s the piece that makes this whole thing work while you sleep.

You already have a workshop inside your community from Day 2. That\'s
your evergreen selling asset.

But let\'s make sure you understand what it\'s actually doing:

Your mini-course or workshop isn\'t meant to \"teach their face off.\"

It\'s meant to shift belief.

Belief in you. Belief in your process. Belief that they can actually do
it.

A powerful asset makes someone think: \"If this is free, imagine what
the paid stuff is like.\"

**What makes it convert:**

-   Starts with story, builds emotional connection

-   Gives simple but real wins

-   Shows the model without giving away the full playbook

-   Reframes what they\'re currently doing (and why it\'s not working)

-   Handles objections before they\'re spoken

-   Makes your paid offer the obvious next step

**Where it lives:**

-   Pinned in your community

-   Linked in your welcome post

-   Shared 1:1 in DMs

-   Referenced in your content loops

-   Re-promoted during evergreen content rotation

Build it once. Use it everywhere. Let it do the selling for you.

## **The \"Always Have A New Offer\" Strategy**

Here\'s where it gets fun.

You don\'t need to create new products to have new promos.

You just need to promote different angles of the same thing.

If you have a stacked offer with multiple components, each component can
become the face of a different promotion.

**Example:**

Your offer includes coaching, a community, a workshop, templates, and DM
scripts.

-   Month 1: Promote the coaching angle. \"Working with 3 people this
    > month on \[specific outcome\].\"

-   Month 2: Promote the workshop. \"Free 5-day training on \[topic\]
    > starting Monday.\"

-   Month 3: Promote the templates. \"Giving away the exact \[asset\] I
    > used to \[result\].\"

Same product. Different front door. Fresh angle every time.

**Why this works:**

-   It plays into the fact that people like new things.

-   You can layer in real scarcity (limited spots, time-sensitive
    > bonuses) without being fake about it.

-   Your content gets hyper-specific because you\'re only talking about
    > one slice.

-   From the outside, it looks like you\'re always releasing something
    > new. But you\'re just selling the same thing smarter.

# **Part 3: Creating Demand (Cash Injections)**

## **When Sales Slow Down**

Even with a solid evergreen system, there will be weeks where things are
quiet.

That\'s normal. It\'s not a reason to panic. It\'s a reason to run a
cash injection.

A cash injection is not a full launch. It\'s a quick, focused push that
creates urgency around your existing offer.

Think of it as putting a fresh coat of paint on what you\'re already
selling.

## **The Cash Injection Framework**

**Step 1: Pick an angle.**

Look at your offer. What piece of it would solve a specific problem your
audience is talking about right now?

Package that as the front end.

**Step 2: Add a layer.**

One of these:

-   Time-sensitive: \"This is available until \[date\].\"

-   Limited spots: \"Working with \[number\] people on this.\"

-   Bonus: \"Next \[number\] people get \[specific thing\].\"

-   Pricing play: \"First \[number\] get \[discount or payment plan\].\"

**Step 3: Test the temperature.**

Put out one piece of content about it. Not a full promo. Just a feeler.

\"Thinking about doing \[thing\] for \[number\] people. Would anyone
want this?\"

If there\'s interest, go.

**Step 4: Run it tight.**

3-7 days max. This is not a prolonged launch. It\'s a sprint.

-   Day 1: Announce it publicly. Clear deadline. Clear limit.

-   Days 2-5: Content around the specific angle. Proof. Stories. What it
    > solves.

-   During: Reach out directly to warm leads. People you\'ve talked to
    > before. People who engaged. Previous buyers.

-   Final Day: Close it publicly. Announce it\'s done. Congratulate
    > whoever got in.

**Step 5: Close the doors and move on.**

Whether it hit or not, close it down publicly.

If it worked: You\'ve got new clients, new momentum, and new marketing
angles.

If it didn\'t: You lost nothing. You put a post out. No harm done.

## **Where To Find Quick Sales**

When you want to inject cash, don\'t start with strangers.

**Work this list in order:**

1.  People you\'ve sent offers to before (on the fence, recently talked)

2.  Folks who raised their hands on content in the last 48 hours

3.  Old, stagnant conversations in your inbox

4.  Community members, followers, story viewers

5.  New people, strangers, outbound

Almost everyone reading this has sales sitting in their inbox right now.
It\'s a matter of finding the right angle and asking.

## **The Monthly Rhythm**

You don\'t need to do a cash injection every week. But having a rhythm
helps.

**Weeks 1-2:** Evergreen content. Build goodwill. Nurture. Free value.
Content loops.

**Week 3:** Test an angle. Soft promo. See what moves.

**Week 4:** If week 3 hit, close it strong with a deadline. If it
didn\'t, go back to evergreen and try a different angle next month.

This gives you the consistency of evergreen selling plus the spikes of
promotional pushes without burning out your audience or yourself.

# **Part 4: Your Daily System**

## **The Simple Ecosystem**

At the end of the day, your business only has 3 moving parts:

1.  **Attention** --- Content that reaches cold people

2.  **Nurture** --- Community and assets that warm them up

3.  **Conversion** --- DMs and conversations that close the sale

That\'s it. Everything else is a distraction.

Your daily job is to feed each of these. Every day. In a focused window.

## **The Daily Rhythm**

This isn\'t about working 12 hours. This is about making every hour
count.

**Block 1: Content (30 minutes)**

-   Create or schedule 1-2 pieces of content

-   One short-form video minimum

-   One written post or story

-   Always point back to the community

If you\'re struggling with what to post, let your business fuel your
content:

-   Conversations you\'re having

-   Results that are happening

-   Questions people are asking

-   Things you\'re doing or learning

Your business IS your content. Keep your eyes open and you\'ll never run
out of ideas.

**Block 2: Community (20 minutes)**

-   Respond to comments and posts inside your group

-   Welcome new members personally

-   Share or highlight a win (yours or a client\'s)

-   Engage with your workshop content

-   Create something / post / value / serve / etc

Your community should feel alive. Not because you\'re babysitting it,
but because you\'re showing up consistently and sparking conversation.

**Block 3: Conversations (30-40 minutes)**

-   Follow up with open conversations

-   Reply to every comment and DM from the last 24 hours

-   Start 2-3 new conversations per day

-   Send at least 1 offer per day

**Inbox Priority:**

1.  People you\'ve already sent offers to

2.  People who engaged on content in the last 48 hours

3.  Old or stagnant conversations

4.  New community members

5.  Story viewers and followers

This is where money is made. Not in the content. Not in the community.
In the conversations.

Content gets attention. Community builds trust. Conversations close
sales.

## **Content Tailored To Audience Temperature**

Not everyone in your audience is at the same stage. Your content should
reflect that.

**Cold (Don\'t know you yet):**

Goal: Create awareness. Get attention. Educate. Entertain.

-   Be relatable. Speak to pain points and challenges they\'re
    > experiencing right now.

-   Educate and inspire. Give small wins that make them think \"if this
    > is free, imagine the paid stuff.\"

-   Build curiosity. Make them want more of you.

-   Keep it simple. No jargon. No assumptions.

-   CTA: Follow, join the group, grab the freebie.

**Warm (Know you, engage, but haven\'t bought):**

Goal: Deepen the relationship. Drive consumption. Move them closer to
buying.

-   Go deeper into your model. Share examples, case studies, more value.

-   Address objections in your content (makes DM conversations easier
    > too).

-   Show proof. Your wins. Client wins. Community wins.

-   Encourage interaction. DMs, lives, Q&As, trainings.

**Hot (On the edge, talked to you, seen the offer):**

Goal: Find the angle that gets them to decide.

-   Highlight the outcome and transformation. What changes when they
    > buy.

-   Make confident offers. Talk price. Stand in the value.

-   Create urgency when possible. Add or remove something. Limited
    > spots.

-   Personalize in DMs. Tailor the offer to what they specifically told
    > you they want.

You don\'t need to create separate content for each temperature every
day. But be aware of who you\'re speaking to. Rotate through the week so
all three get touched.

## **The Metrics That Actually Matter**

Stop tracking followers. Stop obsessing over likes.

**Here\'s what moves money:**

**1. New Community Members Per Week**

This is your pipeline. If this number is flat, revenue will be flat.

**Target:** 50-100 new members per week from consistent content.

Not hitting that? Post more content. More short-form. More aggressive
CTAs pointing to the group.

**2. Community Activity**

A quiet group is a dead group. Engagement is the signal that people are
warming up to buy.

**Target:** 5 posts per week minimum inside your community.

-   1 story post

-   1 engagement post (non-business)

-   1 value or tactical breakdown

-   1 lead gen or 2-step post

-   1 offer post

**3. Conversations Per Day**

If you\'re not talking to people, you\'re not making money.

**Track:** How many new conversations started? How many old ones
rekindled? How many offers sent?

Target: At least 1 offer invitation per day. If you don\'t have the
volume, work backwards. Not enough leads? Post more content.

**4. Content Volume**

Your content is the engine. No posts, no pipeline. No story, no
connection.

**Target:** Minimum 7 short-form videos per week. More is better because
you get data faster. Track what hooks, topics, and formats work best. Do
more of those.

**5. Offers Sent**

If you don\'t ask, the answer is always no.

**Track:** How many times did I invite someone to buy this week?

If you sent 50 offers and made zero sales, it\'s a messaging or
positioning issue. If you sent 2 offers and made zero sales, it\'s a
volume issue.

## **The Flywheel**

Everything feeds everything.

Content brings people in. Community warms them up. Conversations close
them. Results from clients become content. That content brings more
people in.

This is the cycle.

It\'s simple. It\'s boring. It works.

Your job isn\'t to make it complex. Your job is to keep it spinning.

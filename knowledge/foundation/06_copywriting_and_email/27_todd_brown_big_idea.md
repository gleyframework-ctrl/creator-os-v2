## 1. WHY A BIG MARKETING IDEA IS ESSENTIAL

### 1.1 The Attention Crisis

- People are bombarded with thousands of marketing messages daily
- They've become skeptical, guarded, and jaded
- Typical marketing messages are dismissed or ignored

### 1.2 The Failure of "Louder Promises"

Trying to "scream a louder promise" no longer works. Prospects have heard it all before.

### 1.3 Mental Opt-Out

When prospects see a claim they've seen before, they mentally opt out:
"Oh, that. I've heard that. I've seen that."

Then they bounce.

### 1.4 The Categorical Imperative (Bill Bonner)

Individuals subconsciously look for ways to immediately categorize information.

If something looks, sounds, or feels like prior marketing, it gets placed in the "sales pitch" category - and the sales filter goes up.

### 1.5 Differentiation - Better vs. Different

"Better doesn't always get attention. Better doesn't always stand out. Different does."

A Big Marketing Idea differentiates your message.

### 1.6 It's the Secret of the World's Best Marketers

- **Gary Halbert:** "What was important was not [copywriting], it was The Big Marketing Idea."
- **Clayton Makepeace:** His biggest winners were all based on Big Marketing Ideas.
- **Mark Ford:** "Bill Bonner sold more than a billion dollars' worth of publications by doing so."
- **David Ogilvy:** "You will never win fame and fortune unless you invent Big Marketing Ideas."

### 1.7 Big Marketing Idea ≠ Headline

A headline is simply an expression of the underlying idea.

The Big Marketing Idea is the story, the premise, the "news" that compels prospects to stop and engage.

**Example:**

| Book | Idea | Outcome |
|------|------|---------|
| *How to Outsource* | Ordinary idea | Probably didn't sell well |
| *The 4-Hour Work Week* | Big Marketing Idea (escape the 9-5, join the new rich) | NYT bestseller, 1.3M+ copies sold |

Same content, completely different idea.

## 2. THE 10 ELEMENTS OF A BIG MARKETING IDEA

| # | Element | Description |
|---|---------|-------------|
| 1 | Presents One Promise | A bold, audacious, true and believable promise of transformation |
| 2 | It's Specific | Concrete and tangible - specific enough to envision |
| 3 | Conveys One Story | Based on one storyline (discovery, breakthrough, news) |
| 4 | Leads to One Conclusion | One thought you want the prospect to have - one belief |
| 5 | Taps One Emotion | Stimulates one primary emotion (greed, fear, envy, belonging) |
| 6 | Must Be New & Unique | Original - different from what prospects have seen before |
| 7 | Must Feel Timely | Relevant to the here and now - not dated or old |
| 8 | Arresting | Bold, captivating - needs to shock prospects out of complacency |
| 9 | Immediately Understandable | Simple, within their scope of reference |
| 10 | The Rule of One | One single powerful idea - not a mix |

### The Curse of Knowledge

We overestimate what others understand based on our own level of knowledge.

You must communicate at your prospect's level, not your own.

> Simplicity encourages belief. The easier something is to explain and understand, the more belief it generates.

## 3. THE BIG MARKETING IDEA FORMULA

**E.C. (P.P. + U.M.) + I.I. = Big Marketing Idea**

| Abbreviation | Meaning | Role |
|--------------|---------|------|
| E.C. | Emotionally Compelling | The idea stirs emotion |
| P.P. | Primary Promise | The big, overarching benefit |
| U.M. | Unique Mechanism | How the promise is delivered |
| I.I. | Intellectually Interesting | Piques curiosity, feels newsworthy |

Combined, the idea appeals to both heart and head - emotion and logic.

### 3.1 The Primary Promise (P.P.)

The one big, overarching promise of change, transformation, result, or outcome.

**Must be:**
- Big, bold, audacious
- True and believable
- Specific and concrete
- Backed by proof
- Addressing an urgent problem

**How to Find Your Primary Promise:**

| Technique | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Magic Wand Technique | If you could wave a magic wand and grant prospects anything, what would they ask for? |
| Ideal Client Exercise | Imagine your greatest future client success story. What result did they experience? |

### 3.2 The Unique Mechanism (U.M.)

The unique piece, part, component, aspect, process, or system behind your product that delivers the Primary Promise.

**It gives prospects hope** - maybe this time, with this mechanism, they'll get the result.

**It prevents commoditization** and avoids the "Google Marketing Slap."

### 3.3 Three Types of Unique Mechanisms

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Actual Mechanism** | You actually have a unique piece, part, process | FrogTape's Paint Block Technology; P90X's Muscle Confusion |
| **Unspoken Mechanism** | The mechanism isn't unique, but none of your competitors are talking about it | Claude Hopkins' Schlitz beer campaign (sterilization process) |
| **Transubstantiated Mechanism** | Turning something ordinary into something extraordinary by giving it a proprietary name | "The Astronaut's Nutrient" (vitamin D); "Tripwire" (low-cost offer) |

> "Muscle confusion was just a term we came up with... and we sold millions." - Tony Horton, P90X

### 3.4 Intellectually Interesting (I.I.)

The idea is something prospects would want to hear about **even if there were no explicit promise of benefit**.

It piques curiosity, gives a feeling of discovery, an "Aha!" moment.

It feels newsworthy.

## 4. THE PROMISE EXPOSURE SPECTRUM (PES)

Your prospects' "maturity" - what types of claims they've already seen - determines what kind of Big Marketing Idea will work.

### The Five Stages

| Stage | What It Is | Example (Weight-Loss) |
|-------|------------|------------------------|
| **Stage 1: Promise** | The promise itself is new | "Take this pill and you'll lose weight." |
| **Stage 2: Promise Expanded** | Enlarge the promise | "Lose 20 pounds... 30 inches..." |
| **Stage 3: Promise + Unique Mechanism** | Introduce the mechanism | "Lose weight with **Garcinia Cambogia**." |
| **Stage 4: Promise + Expanded Mechanism** | Enlarge the mechanism | "Pharmaceutical-grade Garcinia from the rainforest..." |
| **Stage 5: Prospect Experience** | Focus on prospect's experience | "Tired of dieting... and not losing weight?" |

### Key Insight

- If your idea is too low for where your market is, it will be ignored
- Most marketers get stuck at Stage 2, competing by enlarging promises
- A **Unique Mechanism** lets you stay at Stage 3 longer
- To tap into the **largest, most ignored segment** (Unaware prospects), you need a Big Marketing Idea

### The Prospect Awareness Pyramid

| Level | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Most Aware | Know you and your product - just want the deal |
| Product Aware | Know your product, not sure if it's right |
| Solution Aware | Aware of solutions, evaluating options |
| Problem Aware | Aware of the problem, not solutions |
| Unaware | Not aware of any problem, you, or solutions |

Higher awareness = smaller universe of prospects.

Lower awareness = bigger universe - but you need a Big Marketing Idea to reach them.

## 5. DEVELOPING BIG MARKETING IDEAS

### 5.1 The Process: Digging, Not Inspiration

"You don't get an idea or a headline. You dig it out of the market research. You wring it out of your product. You read. You listen. You experiment. The creativity is not in you. It's in your market. It's in your prospects. It's in your product." - Eugene Schwartz

### 5.2 Research: Be a Bloodhound

- Collect **7 times the information you think you need** (John Caples)
- Research with a hand-truck (Gary Bencivenga)
- Be a bloodhound (Brian Kurtz)
- You need **lots of input** to get **lots of ideas**

### 5.3 Two Types of Information

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| **Specific Information** | Related to your product, prospect, market. Forums, Amazon comments, trade journals, competitor promos |
| **General Information** | Current events, books, magazines, news, movies, podcasts |

> Ideas come from a new combination of specific knowledge + general knowledge.

### 5.4 What Is an Idea?

"An idea is nothing more than a new combination of old, common, seemingly disconnected elements." - James Webb Young

- Look for new or unusual combinations and connections
- The ability to see relationships is a skill that can be developed

### 5.5 Rentier vs. Speculator

| Rentier | Speculator |
|---------|------------|
| Views elements as separate, disconnected bits | Constantly looking for connections and combinations |
| Ignores relationships | Sees each element as another link in the same chain |

> Become a speculator - proactively, intentionally looking for relationships.

### 5.6 The Action Figure Technique

- Combine multiple popular topics into one new story
- "Mutants, ninjas, and turtles were really popular... let's combine all of that." (Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles)

### 5.7 Document Everything

- When researching, don't pre-judge
- Note every idea that strikes your fancy
- Note **proof points** - promises are cheap without proof

### 5.8 Don't Be Afraid to Be Bold

- Explore ideas that are outrageous, controversial, or polarizing
- You're not looking for a tap on the shoulder - you're looking for a whack on the side of the head

### 5.9 Be Patient

- Give yourself time to find interesting stuff and flesh it out
- Don't rush ahead unless you're excited about the idea

### 5.10 Sum It Up in 30 Seconds

- You must be able to articulate your idea in under 30 seconds
- If you can't, you don't have clarity yet

### 5.11 Ideas Have a Limited Window

- What was a Big Marketing Idea three months ago may not be one today
- Never steal old ideas - they existed within a different context

CHUNK: Your Dream Follower and Avatar
CATEGORY: builder
SOURCE: TTB Core Module 01 — Lesson: Your Dream Follower Avatar
---
One of the most powerful shifts you can make as a creator is moving from thinking you are serving an audience to understanding you are connecting with individuals. Yes, thousands of people will see your content — but each one of them is sitting alone on the other side of a screen. They are not a crowd. They are one person.
The Dream Follower and Avatar exercise exists to solve the biggest clarity problem creators face: not knowing exactly who they are talking to. When you try to speak to everyone, you connect with no one. When you speak to one specific person, you end up connecting with thousands of people exactly like them.
Tom Noske describes it this way: "Our goal is to connect with one person on a level that person has never experienced before. And as a result, you will end up connecting with millions of others who are exactly like that person."
---
HOW THE EXERCISE WORKS
You are going to create two real, fully fleshed-out human beings — your Dream Followers. Not demographics. Not audience personas. Actual people with names, ages, backstories, fears, desires, daily lives, and emotional worlds.
Tom's two Dream Followers are Ben (29, male) and Grace (28, female). He has used these same two people for over two years. Every piece of content he makes, he asks: "Would Ben or Grace connect with this?"
Ben's story: A creative who always knew he was meant for more. Dropped out of university, spent six years building something. Works well but feels stuck. Fears that he will never build a real business from his creative pursuits. Passionate about fitness, personal development, physical challenges. Wants financial freedom, a beautiful home, a family. His deepest fear: that he will never be able to provide for the people he loves.
Grace's story: A creative entrepreneur who built an audience around empowering women. Was a high achiever in school because she was a people-pleaser — but always knew that life was not what she wanted. Left law school. Moved out of her parents' home. Started building her own audience. Loves fitness, interior design, the feeling of being at her best. Wants a global brand, books, a podcast, freedom to choose how she spends her time. Her deepest fear: feeling trapped again, returning to a path chosen by others.
---
WHY SPECIFICITY CREATES CONNECTION
The more specific you are about your Dream Follower, the more universal the content feels to the people who match that description. This feels counterintuitive but it is how connection actually works.
When you know that Ben had depression, you can make content about depression. When you know Grace loves interior design, you can reference interior design in a business context. These details are not distractions — they are entry points into the emotional world of your audience.
Tom says: "People comment on my content saying, it feels like you are reading my mind. The reality is I am not. I am just making content for these two very specific people."
---
THE TWO QUESTIONS THAT UNLOCK YOUR CONTENT STRATEGY
Once you have built your two Dream Followers, ask yourself:
1. What stories from my own life would resonate with these two people? Write down as many as you can. Stories of debt. Of failure. Of quitting. Of signing your first client. Of a failed project. Of embarrassment. Of breakthrough. The goal is to build a personal story bank that creates relatability.
2. What content ideas would attract them and repel everyone else? This is not about excluding people. It is about knowing that the content only needs to land for one type of person. When you aim that precisely, the right people feel like you are speaking directly to them.
---
DIAGNOSTIC QUESTION
Before building your next piece of content, can you describe the specific person you are making it for — their name, their age, their biggest fear, and the one emotion they will feel when they see your content?

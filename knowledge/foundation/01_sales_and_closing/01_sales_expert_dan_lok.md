## 1. THE 9-STEP HIGH-TICKET CLOSING SCRIPT

Use this script for every high-ticket sales call.

### Step 1: Building Rapport (2-3 minutes max)
Purpose: Establish connection, set positive tone.

Script:
- How can I help you?
- What can I do for you?
- Why did you book a call?

---

### Step 2: Pre-Frame and Take Charge
Purpose: Establish control; you lead the conversation.

Script:
"OK [Name], I need to ask you some questions about your business to see how we can add value to what you're doing. Is this OK?"

---

### Step 3: Understand the Motivation
Purpose: Uncover the real reason they're on the call.

Script:
- So [Name], what was it that motivated you to invest your time to hop on a call with me today?
- OK, tell me more about that.

---

### Step 4: Identify Their Pains and The #1 Problem
Purpose: Dig deep into their current struggle.

Questions:
- Tell me a bit about your life and what you do.
- How are you currently getting your results?
- What's the biggest problem right now for you?
- How long has this problem been going on?
- Why do you think this problem exists?

KEY PRINCIPLE: If a prospect rates their problem below 5 (on scale of 1-10), use Reverse Psychology to create urgency.

---

### Step 5: Paint the Dream
Purpose: Create emotional vision of their desired future.

Script:
- OK, let's put ourselves one year from today. What would need to happen for you to be truly satisfied and excited?
- What would change in your life if you were living this type of dream?
- What's your motivation for wanting to reach this goal anyway?

---

### Step 6: Bridge the Gap
Purpose: Widen the gap between their current pain and their dream.

Script:
- OK, and what are the biggest obstacles right now between you and that 12-month goal/dream?
- Why do you think you haven't reached that goal already?
- What do you think is stopping you from doing this on your own?

---

### Step 7: The Money Stage (ANCHORING)
Purpose: Get the prospect to name a price first.

Script:
"[Name], thanks for sharing this. I understand the position you are in and we can fix this. I think you're a good fit. Just out of curiosity, let's pretend we could do X for you. What do you think would be a reasonable investment to get this kind of return?"

WAIT. Let them speak first.

Then: "OK [Name], well unfortunately we charge more than that. I am sure by working together I could get you from [pains] and help you get [promised land]. Would you be comfortable spending [X] more a month to make what you described a reality?"

---

### Step 8: Moving to the Close
Purpose: Confirm commitment and timeline.

Script:
- OK [Name], when do you want to fix this problem and get things moving in the right direction?
- How committed would you say you are to make this happen?

---

### Step 9: Signed, Sealed and Delivered
Purpose: Ask for the close and set immediate next steps.

Script:
"OK [Name], my assistant will send you an agreement. I need you to get that back to me TODAY. Can you do that for me? Good, I look forward to working with you."

## 2. DAN LOK'S CORE SELLING PRINCIPLES

### The 7 Most Dangerous Words
"My business is different." - This kills progress.

### Lokism
"Any moron can quickly come up with lots of reasons to invalidate ideas. Most do. There's genius and profit ONLY in coming up with ways to apply ideas."

### Selling is About Power
"Traditionally and logically, it is the prospect who has the power. For selling to be easy, price to be irrelevant, entrepreneurs must take all the power away from the buyer. The seller has to be perceived by the buyer as having all the power."

### People Buy Feelings
1. People buy BY feelings (emotions)
2. People ACTUALLY buy feelings
3. People buy in response to YOUR feelings
4. People buy by bonding with the seller, SHARED feelings

## 4. THE GEM THEORY - PERSONALITY SELLING

Use this to identify personality types and adapt your sales approach.

### EMERALD (D-Type)
- Motivated by: Facts, figures, systems, organization, research, clear direction, results
- Pet peeves: Tardiness, not following protocol, incorrect grammar/typos
- How to win: Provide all details. Be on time. Be precise, exact, planned. Show confidence.
- Language: "Here are the facts and figures." / "This is the most efficient system."

### RUBY (B-Type)
- Motivated by: Challenges, goals, pressure, competition, public recognition, big dreams
- Pet peeves: Complacency, lack of drive
- How to win: Impress them by being an idea generator. Let them go first. Challenge them.
- Language: "Let me show you how it's done." / "This is a challenge."

### SAPPHIRE (A-Type)
- Motivated by: Fun, public recognition, positive energy, human interaction
- Pet peeves: Rules, structure, schedules, negativity
- How to win: Praise their successes. Be positive. Approach as a fun-loving friend.
- Language: "Trust me, it works." / "This will be fun."

### PEARL (C-Type)
- Motivated by: Being part of something bigger, helping others, authenticity, trust
- Pet peeves: Confrontation, disingenuous people, loud environments, pressure
- How to win: Be authentic. Appeal to their good nature. Share how you've helped others.
- Language: "I want to help you." / "This will make a difference."

## 5. PRICE TIMING RULES

### Never reveal price upfront
Build value first. If they ask early, redirect:
"It depends. Can I ask you a few questions first to understand what you're looking for?"

### Give price range mid-call
"The investment typically ranges from $X to $Y depending on your specific setup. Is that within your budget range?"

### Late in call (qualified)
Anchor with value, then give price + follow-up question.

## 6. OBJECTION HANDLING TECHNIQUES

### The "If I could show you a way to get this for free" Technique
When you get a price objection:
"If I could show you a way to get this for free, would you take it?"

This shifts their mindset from cost to value. Once they say yes, explain that the "free" way is the one that pays for itself (ROI).

### Dan's Golden Rule
Anything but a YES is a NO. Never let a call linger in the "maybe" zone.

## 7. 50 MUST-HAVE SALES QUESTIONS

### Revenue Goals
- How did you arrive at that goal?
- Who or what was involved in coming up with that goal?
- What's the upside (or downside) to missing, meeting, or exceeding that goal?
- When do you need to achieve these goals by?

### Business Goals
- What's at the top of your priority list for the rest of this year?
- How did those things make it to the top of the list?
- What problem are you trying to solve?
- What metrics are you responsible for?
- Are there specific revenue goals you're working toward?

### Budget and Decision Makers
- Is this problem funded?
- Whose budget does the funding come from?
- Who else will be involved in choosing a vendor?
- What's the process for purchasing once you decide?
- How can I help make this easy?

### Timeline and Consequences
- What's your timeline to achieve these goals?
- If it took you an additional month, three months, six months to get there, is that ok?
- What would a successful outcome look like?
- How will this solution make your life better?
